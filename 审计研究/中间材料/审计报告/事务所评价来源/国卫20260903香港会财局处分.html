AFRC reprimands HLB Hodgson Impey Cheng Limited and its two directors for multiple audit deficiencies and imposes fines of HK$910,000

  • 3 September 2026

The Accounting and Financial Reporting Council (AFRC) has sanctioned the following PIE auditor and its two practising directors for multiple audit deficiencies identified in the audit of the consolidated financial statements of Sound Global Ltd. (Company)1 and its subsidiaries (collectively, Group) for the year ended 31 December 2014 (2014 Audit):

 

  • HLB Hodgson Impey Cheng Limited (HLB),2 auditor for the 2014 Audit;

 

  • Mr Yu Chi Fat (Yu, together with HLB, Auditor), engagement director for the 2014 Audit; and

 

  • Mr Shek Lui (Shek), engagement quality control reviewer for the 2014 Audit.

 

In this case, despite being aware of fraud risks in relation to opening bank balances in the 2014 Audit, the Auditor failed to maintain control over the confirmation process and failed to design and perform adequate audit procedures to verify the existence of opening bank balances, demonstrating a lack of professional scepticism and due care in carrying out the audit.

 

The AFRC has issued a public reprimand to each of HLB, Yu and Shek (collectively, Regulatees), and imposed pecuniary penalties totalling HK$910,000, comprising penalties of HK$490,000 for HLB, HK$350,000 for Yu and HK$70,000 for Shek.3

 

Background

 

This case originated from a complaint by a local financial regulator in 2016 based on allegations in a short seller’s report published in February 2015 (Short Seller’s Report) that the Company had falsified its financial statements by inflating revenue and bank balances between 2011 and 2013.  HLB was appointed as the Company’s auditor in July 2015, after the Company’s predecessor auditor resigned following its identification of the potential audit issues arising from a cash shortfall of approximately RMB 2 billion between the Company’s books and bank balances (Potential Audit Issues) and the associated fraud risks.  At the time of HLB’s appointment, it was aware of the Short Seller’s Report and the Potential Audit Issues.

 

The AFRC’s key findings

 

As the incoming auditor undertaking this first-year audit engagement, with knowledge of the potential fraud risks, the Auditor should have, but failed to, exercise professional scepticism, design and perform robust audit procedures to address any red flags associated with the potential fraud risks.

 

The Auditor sought to rely on external confirmations to ascertain the existence of opening bank balances as at 1 January 2014 (Opening Bank Balances).  When performing external confirmation procedures, auditors should maintain control over the confirmation process to ensure reliability of the confirmations, which can be achieved if auditors receive the confirmations directly from the confirming parties.  However, of the 129 bank confirmations received, there is no evidence based on the audit working papers as to from whom 126 confirmations were received, nor how the Auditor verified their authenticity.  These 126 confirmations accounted for over 99% of the Group’s Opening Bank Balances.

 

Although the Auditor had purportedly designed and performed additional audit procedures in response to the Potential Audit Issues, such procedures were insufficient to verify the existence of the Opening Bank Balances.  For example, while the Auditor had allegedly reviewed information on the computer screens of bank staff during bank visits, and called general lines of bank branches to confirm the balances, such procedures were inherently impracticable.  The Auditor’s deficiency was compounded by the lack of sufficient documentation of such additional procedures in the audit working papers.  For instance, no details such as the specific dates, times, or locations of bank visits were recorded.  Consequently, neither the bank confirmations received nor the additional procedures purportedly performed provided the Auditor with sufficient appropriate audit evidence to support the existence of the Group’s Opening Bank Balances.

 

A similar issue was also identified in the Auditor’s work concerning opening balances of amounts due from/to customers for contract work and trade receivables.  Based on the audit working papers, there is no evidence as to from whom the confirmations were received, nor how the Auditor verified their authenticity, and there was likewise a lack of sufficient documentation of the additional audit procedures purportedly performed.

 

In relation to revenue from turnkey projects and services, the Auditor failed to obtain an understanding and evaluate the reasonableness of management’s construction revenue estimates.  More specifically, the audit working papers did not refer to any construction contracts or progress reports, nor did they contain summaries documenting the key terms of each contract.

 

The AFRC’s disciplinary decision

 

In deciding the appropriate disciplinary sanctions, the AFRC has considered all the relevant circumstances, including the nature, seriousness, duration, frequency and impact of the misconduct.  In particular, the AFRC considered the Auditor’s failure to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to verify the existence of the Opening Bank Balances to be serious, given that the Auditor was aware of the existence of potential fraud risks and the Potential Audit Issues.  Such failure demonstrated the Auditor’s lack of professional scepticism and due care in carrying out the audit, as well as over-reliance on management representations.  The AFRC has further taken into account the cooperation provided by the Regulatees.  In light of the Regulatees’ full admission of breaches and their early settlement with the AFRC, the AFRC has exercised its discretion to reduce the pecuniary penalties in accordance with the Guidance Note on Cooperation with the AFRC.

 

Mr Denis Cheng, Head of Investigation and Compliance, said, “Where fraud risks or other significant red flags are identified, whether from public sources or from the predecessor auditor, auditors must remain highly vigilant to such risks and design and perform procedures that appropriately address the risks.  Failure to do so, particularly where such risks were known, undermines audit quality and exposes firms and auditors to significant regulatory consequences.”

 

Ms Hester Leung, Head of Discipline, said, “This case reflects serious shortcomings on the part of the incoming auditor in appropriately assessing and responding to red flags and heightened fraud risks relating to a listed entity that was already subject to significant concerns, despite having been put on notice of those concerns and risks at the time of appointment.  It is of note that this case was assessed under the old regime where the maximum pecuniary penalty for a misconduct was HK$500,000, whereas if similar misconduct were to arise in PIE audit engagements on or after 1 October 2019, a substantially more severe sanction could be expected under the current regulatory regime.”

 

The AFRC appreciates the continuing assistance and support of the Supervision and Evaluation Bureau of the Ministry of Finance of the People’s Republic of China in obtaining relevant audit working papers in this case, and will continue to work closely with relevant authorities to strengthen cross-boundary cooperation and to enhance effective regulatory oversight and enforcement.

 

For details of the decision, please refer to the Statement of Disciplinary Action.
 

[1] Previous stock code: 00967, now delisted. The Group was principally engaged in environmental construction related to water treatment.

[2] Corporate practice registration number: M0402.

[3] As the 2014 Audit was completed prior to 1 October 2019, the Regulatees were regarded as professional persons and sanctioned pursuant to section 37CA of the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council Ordinance for committing a CPA misconduct.  For each CPA misconduct, the AFRC may order a regulatee to pay a pecuniary penalty of an amount not exceeding HK$500,000.
